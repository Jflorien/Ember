01 — THE ORGANISING PRINCIPLE

Heat is state.

Every design system needs one rule that decides the hundred small questions nobody wrote down. Here it is: cold stone is inert; anything alive, available or happening is heated. Colour is not decoration in Ember — it is the readout.

Cold

Basalt and iron. Spent spell slots, inactive turns, empty equipment slots, unavailable actions, dead tokens, past log entries. The default state of the interface is unlit.

Warm

Ember at partial intensity. Resources you still hold, conditions running, quests active, items equipped. Present, but not demanding your attention this second.

White-hot

Forge gold with a glow. It is your turn. A critical landed. A legendary dropped. Reserved so completely that when it appears, every head at the table turns.

Why this, for this product

Three surfaces read the same state at different distances — a DM leaning into a laptop, a player glancing at a phone, and four people looking at a television across a room. A system built on hue alone fails at the third distance. A system built on luminance and glow survives it: heat is visible when detail is not. That is why the accent is light-emitting rather than flat, and why the palette's cold end is genuinely dark rather than mid-grey.

It also comes out of your own material. The two strongest elemental threads in the handbook are fire (Ash-Blooded, Path of the Wildfire — Flameburst Rage, Burning Steps, Cinder Skin, Inferno Surge) and stone (Stoneborn, "living rock-folk", the Petrified condition). Fire against stone is lava. The system is named from your content, not imposed on it.

03 — TYPOGRAPHY

Carved, set, and counted.

Three roles, and a strict rule about numbers. Display type is chiselled and used sparingly. Interface type is a neutral grotesque that gets out of the way. And every number that can change during play is monospaced and tabular, so it never shifts as it counts.

Display — Cinzel
Howl of Ruin

Roman carved capitals — the letterform of things cut into stone. Wordmark, page titles, class and spell names in hero positions. Never below 20px, never for more than one line of running text.

Interface — Inter
Flameburst Rage

Everything a player reads to make a decision: rules text, labels, descriptions, buttons. Weights 400 / 600 / 650 only. Tight tracking on headings, normal on body.

Data — JetBrains Mono
2d6+4 · DC 15

Every HP value, modifier, dice expression, DC, slot count and timestamp. Tabular numerals are mandatory — a HP counter that reflows as it ticks reads as broken.

The runic label

The system's most repeated element, and the thing that will make it recognisable. Mono, 10.5px, 600 weight, 0.18em tracking, uppercase, ash-500. It marks every section, every field and every stat — an inscription above the thing it names. Gold variant for section openers only.

04 — FORM LANGUAGE

The cut corner.

Nothing in Ember has a rounded corner. Surfaces are chamfered on two opposing corners — top-left and bottom-right — as though cut from plate. It is one decision, applied without exception, and it does more for recognisability than any amount of ornament.

Plate · 12px
Raised · 8px
Hot edge
Edges catch light

Borders are a 1px gradient, brighter at the top-left and dark at the bottom-right — metal lit from above. Never a flat grey stroke.

Depth is hard

Elevation uses tight, dark shadow and inset rims rather than soft blur. Stone does not diffuse. Blur is reserved for glow.

Grids are 4px

4 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 48. Chamfers scale with the surface: 6px on chips, 8px on controls, 12px on panels.

05 — MOTION

Struck, then cooling.

Two curves. Impacts are fast and hard on the way in. Recovery is long and eased on the way out. Nothing in this product should feel bouncy — it is iron, not rubber.

Strike · 110ms

Damage flash, dice land, button press. cubic-bezier(.2,0,.15,1)

Cool · 520ms

HP draining, glow fading, panels settling. cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1)

Breathe · 2.4s

Active turn, concentration, artifact rarity. Opacity only — never scale.

Count

Numbers roll to their new value over 400ms. They never jump, and never reflow.

Reduced motion removes every breathe and every roll, and collapses strikes to an instant state change. Nothing in the system carries meaning that only exists in the animation — the glow, the border and the number all say the same thing.

09 — SESSION

What just happened.

The session log is the player-facing face of the event stream. Every row is one committed event — which is why the log needs no separate data model, and why it can be filtered, replayed and cited in a recap.

▼21:05 Sela takes 11 bludgeoning

Narration

The Dungeon Master

The room goes white. When it comes back, the goblins are scattered like kicked coals and the crates are burning where they stand. Somewhere behind the smoke, something much heavier begins to move.

Narration is the one surface that gets generous measure and line height — it is prose, and it is meant to be read aloud. Everything else in the product is scanned.

10 — CONTROLS

Forge, iron, ghost.

One heated button per view, at most. If two things glow, neither is the answer.

11 — THE THREE SURFACES

Same system, three distances.

The tokens do not change between surfaces. Density, scale and how much heat is permitted do.

/dm — arm's length

DM Console

Highest density in the product. Base 13px, tighter spacing, more per screen. Hidden information carries a permanent dm_only rim in iron so it can never be confused with what the table can see. Heat is used sparingly — the DM is looking at everything at once.

/play — in the hand

Player App

Touch first: 44px minimum targets, base 15px, one column. The stat frame is pinned and everything else scrolls beneath it. This is the only surface where a person is looking down instead of up — brightness lowered accordingly.

/table — across the room

Table View

Everything scales ×1.6. No text under 18px, no interactive affordances at all, no hover states. Heat is unrestricted here — this is the surface the whole system was tuned for. Assume a badly-calibrated television.

12 — DO & DON'T

How to not wreck it.

Do
  • Let cold be the default. Restraint at the dark end is what makes the heat land.
  • Use one heated element per view — the thing you want them to do next.
  • Put every changing number in tabular mono.
  • Pair every colour signal with a shape, a label or a border weight.
  • Scale the chamfer with the surface, never the other way round.
  • Reserve gold for turn state, concentration and legendary. Nothing else.
Don't
  • Round a corner. Not once, not for a modal, not for an avatar.
  • Use molten for body copy, links, or anything a player reads a paragraph of.
  • Add a second display typeface. The system has one, and it is used rarely.
  • Animate anything with scale or bounce — this world is iron.
  • Introduce a new semantic colour without deleting one.
  • Use texture or parchment imagery. The fantasy here comes from light, not from props.
13 — IMPLEMENTATION

Tokens.

These are already wired into the Ember scaffold as CSS custom properties and Tailwind theme extensions. Component classes live in the components layer so utilities always win.

/* globals.css */
:root{
  /* basalt — surfaces */
  --basalt-990:#06070a; --basalt-900:#0d0f15;
  --basalt-850:#12141c; --basalt-800:#171a23;
  --basalt-700:#232833; --basalt-600:#2d3340;
  /* molten — the heat ramp */
  --molten-700:#a8380c; --molten-600:#d14e11;
  --molten-500:#f26419; --molten-400:#ff8534;
  /* forge — highlight */
  --forge-500:#ffb627;  --forge-400:#ffd166;
  /* ash — type */
  --ash-500:#565d70; --ash-300:#8d96ab;
  --ash-100:#dfe4ee;
  /* form */
  --chamfer:12px;
}

/* the surface primitive — two cut corners, gradient edge */
.plate{
  clip-path:polygon(var(--chamfer) 0, 100% 0,
    100% calc(100% - var(--chamfer)), calc(100% - var(--chamfer)) 100%, 0 100%, 0 var(--chamfer));
}

The chamfer is a clip-path, and the 1px edge is a masked gradient layered behind it — which is why borders survive the cut instead of being clipped away with it. That single technique is the whole form language; everything else is colour and restraint.
